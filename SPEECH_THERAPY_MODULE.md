# Speech Therapy Clinical Module

## Overview

Comprehensive clinical documentation module for speech-language pathologists providing home health services. Supports all required ST documentation types with structured data collection for speech, swallowing, cognition, and language assessment.

## Document Types

### 1. ST Evaluation
Initial comprehensive assessment performed within 5 days of admission.

**Purpose:** Establish baseline speech, language, cognitive, and swallowing function; identify deficits; set therapy goals; create treatment plan.

**Sections:**
1. **Speech Assessment** - Articulation, voice quality, fluency, intelligibility
2. **Swallowing Assessment** - Dysphagia evaluation, FOIS level, aspiration risk, diet recommendations
3. **Cognitive Evaluation** - Alertness, orientation, attention, memory, problem-solving
4. **Communication Abilities** - Comprehension, expression, aphasia type, AAC needs
5. **Therapy Goals** - Short-term and long-term functional communication/swallowing goals
6. **Treatment Plan** - Interventions, frequency, duration, skilled need justification

**Key Fields:**
- Referral diagnosis and onset date
- Articulation status and error patterns
- Voice quality and volume
- Fluency and intelligibility percentage
- Dysphagia presence and onset
- Oral and pharyngeal phase function
- Aspiration risk level
- FOIS (Functional Oral Intake Scale) level
- Current diet and liquid consistency
- Aphasia type classification
- Auditory and reading comprehension
- Verbal and written expression
- Cognitive-linguistic skills
- AAC recommendations
- SMART format therapy goals
- Treatment frequency and duration

**Estimated Time:** 90 minutes  
**Required Frequency:** Once at admission

---

### 2. ST Visit Note
Documentation for routine treatment sessions.

**Purpose:** Document skilled interventions provided, patient response, and progress toward communication/swallowing goals.

**Sections:**
1. **Visit Details** - Date, time in/out, visit number
2. **Interventions Performed** - Specific treatments provided this visit
3. **Patient Response** - Tolerance, attention, participation, cueing needs
4. **Progress Toward Goals** - Status of each functional goal
5. **Plan Updates** - Changes to treatment, diet modifications, next visit focus

**Key Fields:**
- Interventions: speech exercises, language therapy, cognitive therapy, dysphagia therapy, compensatory strategies, AAC training, caregiver education, home program
- Activity tolerance
- Attention during session
- Patient participation level
- Cueing required (independent to maximal)
- Goal progress status
- Diet/liquid consistency changes
- Plan modifications

**Estimated Time:** 30 minutes  
**Required Frequency:** Every visit

---

### 3. ST Progress Note
Periodic comprehensive reassessment of patient progress.

**Purpose:** Summarize progress over time period, reassess functional status in all domains, update goals and treatment plan.

**Sections:**
1. **Progress Summary** - Overall progress rating, visits completed
2. **Current Status** - Objective status for speech, language, cognition, swallowing
3. **Goals Status** - Progress toward each short-term and long-term goal
4. **Treatment Plan Changes** - Frequency changes, treatment focus adjustments

**Key Fields:**
- Period start date
- Total visits completed
- Overall progress rating
- Current speech status
- Current language status
- Current cognitive status
- Current swallow status
- Current FOIS level
- Current functional communication rating
- Short-term goals status
- Long-term goals status
- New or revised goals
- Frequency change recommendations
- Diet/liquid advancement or restrictions
- Continued skilled need justification

**Estimated Time:** 45 minutes  
**Required Frequency:** Every 30 days (Medicare requirement)

---

### 4. ST Discharge Summary
Final documentation at end of ST services.

**Purpose:** Document outcomes, summarize episode, provide recommendations for continued communication and safe swallowing.

**Sections:**
1. **Discharge Information** - Date, reason for discharge, total visits
2. **Status at Admission** - Baseline functional levels
3. **Status at Discharge** - Final functional levels
4. **Goals and Outcomes** - Achievement summary
5. **Recommendations** - Home program, compensatory strategies, AAC, diet recommendations, follow-up care

**Key Fields:**
- Discharge date and reason
- Total visits completed
- Admission speech, language, cognition, swallow, communication status
- Discharge speech, language, cognition, swallow, communication status
- Goals achievement (all/most/some/minimal/none met)
- Home exercise program provided
- Compensatory strategies taught
- AAC device recommendations
- Diet and swallow strategy recommendations
- Follow-up care recommendations
- Ongoing safety concerns
- Overall discharge summary

**Estimated Time:** 60 minutes  
**Required Frequency:** Once at discharge

## Clinical Assessment Areas

### Speech Assessment

**Articulation:**
- Within Normal Limits
- Mildly/Moderately/Severely Impaired
- Document specific error patterns (e.g., /r/, /s/, final consonants)

**Voice Quality:**
- Normal
- Hoarse, Breathy, Harsh, Strained, Weak
- Document volume (adequate/reduced/increased/variable)

**Fluency:**
- Fluent
- Dysfluent (stuttering, cluttering)

**Intelligibility:**
- 100% - Always intelligible
- 75% - Usually intelligible
- 50% - Sometimes intelligible
- 25% - Rarely intelligible
- 0% - Unintelligible

---

### Swallowing Assessment

**FOIS - Functional Oral Intake Scale:**
- **Level 1:** NPO (Nothing by Mouth)
- **Level 2:** Tube dependent with minimal PO
- **Level 3:** Tube dependent with consistent PO
- **Level 4:** Total PO of single consistency
- **Level 5:** Total PO of multiple consistencies (requires special preparation)
- **Level 6:** Total PO with modified diet (no special preparation)
- **Level 7:** Total PO with no restrictions

**Diet Levels (IDDSI Framework):**
- Level 4: Puree
- Level 5: Minced & Moist
- Level 6: Soft & Bite-Sized
- Level 7: Regular

**Liquid Consistency:**
- Thin liquids
- Nectar thick
- Honey thick
- Pudding thick
- No oral liquids

**Aspiration Risk:**
- None/Minimal
- Mild Risk
- Moderate Risk
- Severe Risk

**Phases:**
- **Oral Phase:** Chewing, bolus formation, tongue movement
- **Pharyngeal Phase:** Swallow initiation, pharyngeal contraction, laryngeal elevation

**Instrumental Evaluation:**
- **FEES:** Fiberoptic Endoscopic Evaluation of Swallowing
- **VFSS:** Videofluoroscopic Swallow Study (Modified Barium Swallow)

---

### Cognitive-Linguistic Assessment

**Alertness:**
- Alert & Oriented
- Lethargic
- Somnolent
- Fluctuating

**Orientation:**
- x4 (Person, Place, Time, Situation)
- x3, x2, x1, x0 (Disoriented)

**Attention:**
- Within Normal Limits
- Mildly/Moderately/Severely Impaired

**Memory:**
- **Short-Term Memory:** Immediate recall
- **Long-Term Memory:** Remote information

**Executive Function:**
- Problem-solving
- Planning
- Reasoning
- Judgment

**Safety Awareness:**
- Intact
- Impaired
- Absent

### Language/Communication Assessment

**Aphasia Types:**
- **None:** No aphasia
- **Broca's (Expressive):** Poor fluency, relatively intact comprehension
- **Wernicke's (Receptive):** Fluent but poor comprehension
- **Global:** Severe impairment in all modalities
- **Anomic:** Word-finding difficulties
- **Conduction:** Impaired repetition
- **Transcortical:** Various subtypes

**Comprehension:**
- **Auditory:** Understanding spoken language
- **Reading:** Understanding written language

**Expression:**
- **Verbal:** Speaking
- **Written:** Writing

**Additional Skills:**
- **Naming:** Ability to name objects/people
- **Repetition:** Ability to repeat words/sentences
- **Pragmatics:** Social communication skills

**Functional Communication Rating:**
- Independent
- Mostly Effective
- Partially Effective
- Minimally Effective
- Non-functional

**Alternative/Augmentative Communication (AAC):**
- Not needed
- Gestures/Pointing
- Picture board
- Speech generating device
- Writing/Typing
- Recommended for trial

## Treatment Interventions

### Speech Therapy Interventions
- **Articulation exercises:** Sound production practice
- **Voice therapy:** Vocal hygiene, breath support
- **Fluency shaping:** Rate control, easy onset
- **Intelligibility drills:** Overarticulation, pacing

### Language Therapy (Aphasia)
- **Naming therapy:** Word retrieval strategies
- **Sentence production:** Grammar, syntax practice
- **Auditory comprehension:** Following directions, yes/no questions
- **Reading/writing:** Functional literacy tasks

### Cognitive-Linguistic Therapy
- **Attention training:** Sustained, divided, selective attention
- **Memory strategies:** Mnemonics, spaced retrieval
- **Problem-solving:** Functional scenarios
- **Executive function:** Planning, organization

### Dysphagia Therapy
- **Oral motor exercises:** Strengthening, ROM
- **Swallow maneuvers:** Supraglottic, Mendelsohn, effortful swallow
- **Compensatory strategies:** Chin tuck, head turn, small sips
- **Diet progression:** Gradual advancement with reassessment
- **Sensory stimulation:** Thermal-tactile, taste

### Compensatory Strategies
- **Communication strategies:** Gestures, writing, key words
- **Environmental modifications:** Reduce distractions, optimize lighting
- **Cueing hierarchies:** Verbal, visual, tactile prompts
- **Swallow strategies:** Positioning, texture modification

### AAC Training
- **Low-tech:** Communication boards, picture exchange
- **High-tech:** Speech generating devices, tablets with communication apps
- **Training:** Device operation, message creation, partner training

### Caregiver Education
- **Communication strategies:** How to support patient
- **Swallow safety:** Recognizing signs of aspiration
- **Home program:** Exercises, strategies to practice
- **Emergency procedures:** Choking protocols

## Goal Setting

### SMART Goals Format
All therapy goals must be:
- **Specific:** Clearly defined functional outcome
- **Measurable:** Objective criteria for achievement
- **Achievable:** Realistic given patient condition
- **Relevant:** Meaningful to patient's life
- **Time-bound:** Target date for achievement

### Example Goals

**Speech Goal:**
> "Patient will produce target sounds /s/, /r/, /l/ with 80% accuracy at word level during structured practice with minimal cueing by 4/15/2026."

**Language Goal (Aphasia):**
> "Patient will use compensatory strategies (gestures, writing, communication board) to express basic needs and wants with 90% success in functional situations by 5/1/2026."

**Cognitive Goal:**
> "Patient will follow 3-step commands with moderate cueing during functional tasks (meal prep, medication management) with 75% accuracy by 4/20/2026."

**Swallowing Goal:**
> "Patient will advance from FOIS Level 5 to Level 6 (total PO with modified diet) while maintaining aspiration-free swallowing as evidenced by clinical assessment by 5/1/2026."

**Functional Communication Goal:**
> "Patient will communicate basic needs using AAC device with minimal cueing achieving 'mostly effective' functional communication rating by discharge on 5/15/2026."

## Regulatory Compliance

### Medicare Requirements

**Documentation Timing:**
- ST Evaluation within 5 days of admission
- Visit notes for every skilled visit
- Progress notes every 30 days minimum
- Discharge summary at discharge

**Skilled Need Justification:**
Every document must clearly explain why skilled ST services are necessary:
- Complexity requires SLP expertise and training
- Patient/caregiver cannot manage independently or safely
- Teaching complex swallow strategies or compensatory techniques
- Ongoing assessment and treatment plan modification needed
- Safety concerns (aspiration risk, communication barriers)

**Functional Focus:**
Documentation must emphasize functional outcomes:
- ✓ "Patient advanced from FOIS Level 3 to Level 5, now taking modified diet orally"
- ✗ "Patient's pharyngeal phase improved"
- ✓ "Patient now uses picture board to communicate needs with 80% success"
- ✗ "Patient's naming ability improved"

### Required Elements

**Every Document Must Include:**
- Patient identification
- Date of service
- Clinician name and credentials (CCC-SLP)
- Skilled interventions provided
- Patient response and performance
- Progress (or lack thereof)
- Plan for continued care
- Signature

**Evaluation Must Include:**
- Medical diagnosis and onset
- Speech, language, cognitive, and swallowing assessment
- Objective measurements and functional status
- Safety assessment (aspiration risk, cognitive safety)
- Functional goals (ST and LT)
- Treatment plan with frequency/duration
- Rehabilitation potential
- Skilled need justification

## Safety Considerations

### Aspiration Risk Management

**High-Risk Indicators:**
- Wet/gurgly voice quality
- Coughing during/after swallowing
- Throat clearing
- History of aspiration pneumonia
- Silent aspiration (no overt signs)

**Safety Measures:**
- Appropriate diet texture modifications
- Liquid consistency thickening
- Compensatory strategies (chin tuck, head turn)
- Supervised meals
- Caregiver education on signs of aspiration
- Emergency protocols in place

### Cognitive Safety Concerns

**Risk Factors:**
- Impaired judgment
- Poor safety awareness
- Memory deficits affecting medication/diet compliance
- Inability to follow swallow precautions

**Safety Interventions:**
- Caregiver supervision recommendations
- Environmental modifications
- Written reminders/cues
- Simplified instructions
- Regular reassessment

6. Sign electronically when complete

### Best Workflow

**Initial Evaluation:**
1. Complete Speech Assessment (articulation, voice, fluency)
2. Perform Swallowing Assessment (clinical swallow exam, determine FOIS level)
3. Conduct Cognitive Evaluation (orientation, memory, attention)
4. Assess Communication Abilities (comprehension, expression, aphasia type)
5. Establish SMART goals based on findings
6. Create treatment plan with skilled need justification
7. Review for completeness and sign

**Routine Visit Note:**
1. Complete Visit Details (date, times)
2. Check off interventions performed
3. Document patient response, cueing needs, tolerance
4. Update goal progress
5. Note any diet changes or plan modifications
6. Sign immediately after visit

**Progress Note (every 30 days):**
1. Review all visits since last progress note
2. Perform reassessment of all domains (speech, language, cognition, swallow)
3. Document current FOIS level and compare to baseline
4. Update goal status (achieved/progressing/revised)
5. Justify continued skilled need
6. Recommend frequency changes or diet advancement if appropriate
7. Discuss discharge planning if goals nearly met

**Discharge Summary:**
1. Complete on date of last visit
2. Document reason for discharge
3. Compare admission vs discharge status for all domains
4. Summarize goal achievement
5. Provide home program and compensatory strategies
6. Include diet/swallow recommendations
7. Document any ongoing safety concerns

## Best Practices

### Documentation Tips

1. **Be Specific:** Use exact percentages, FOIS levels, cue levels
   - ✓ "Patient swallows nectar thick liquids with chin tuck strategy with no overt signs of aspiration"
   - ✗ "Patient swallows safely"

2. **Use Objective Data:** Include measurable information
   - ✓ "Intelligibility improved from 50% to 75% at conversational level"
   - ✗ "Speech improved"

3. **Document Skilled Need:** Explain WHY SLP is required
   - ✓ "Skilled ST needed for assessment of swallow safety during diet upgrades and teaching of compensatory swallow strategies to reduce aspiration risk"
   - ✗ "ST for dysphagia therapy"

4. **Show Progress:** Compare to baseline and goals
   - ✓ "Patient advanced from FOIS Level 3 (baseline) to Level 5, progressing toward goal of Level 6"
   - ✗ "Patient on FOIS Level 5"

5. **Address Setbacks:** Explain lack of progress or regression
   - ✓ "Patient regressed from FOIS Level 5 to Level 4 following hospitalization for pneumonia; skilled ST resumed for reassessment and modified dysphagia therapy"
   - ✗ "Patient not progressing"

### Common Pitfalls to Avoid

❌ **Vague Descriptions:** "Some improvement noted"  
✓ **Specific Statements:** "Functional communication rating improved from 'minimally effective' to 'partially effective'"

❌ **Impairment Focus:** "Pharyngeal phase is impaired"  
✓ **Functional Focus:** "Impaired pharyngeal phase results in aspiration risk requiring modified diet and compensatory swallow strategies"

❌ **Missing Skilled Need:** "Continue ST per plan"  
✓ **Clear Justification:** "Skilled ST needed for ongoing assessment of aspiration risk during diet progression and modification of therapy techniques based on patient response"

❌ **Ignoring Safety Concerns:** Documentation does not mention aspiration risk  
✓ **Safety-Focused:** "Moderate aspiration risk persists; patient/caregiver educated on signs of aspiration; meal supervision recommended; emergency protocols reviewed"

### Common Questions

**Q: How do I document a FEES or VFSS?**  
A: Select "FEES" or "VFSS" in the instrumental evaluation field, then summarize key findings in the narrative, including presence/timing of aspiration, residue, and recommendations.

**Q: When should I recommend an AAC device?**  
A: When verbal communication is severely impaired and unlikely to recover sufficiently for functional communication, or when compensatory communication methods would significantly improve quality of life.

**Q: How do I advance a patient's diet?**  
A: Document clinical assessment showing improved swallow safety, reduced aspiration risk, and successful trials with more advanced textures. Update FOIS level and diet/liquid recommendations accordingly.

**Q: What if cognitive deficits prevent participation in therapy?**  
A: Document cognitive limitations, focus on caregiver education, implement compensatory strategies, and adjust goals to realistic functional outcomes with support.
